VoiceRelay: A Live Bluetooth Mic to Speaker Relay with Its Own Echo Canceller

A native Android app that carries a voice from any microphone to any speaker in real time. The audio engine is C++ on Oboe, and the echo canceller was built from scratch.

androidkotlinjetpack composec++oboedsp

The problem

Carrying a voice further usually means a PA system. For an announcement, a classroom or a shop floor, a phone already has the Bluetooth radio and the processing power. But Android does not make it easy to record from one Bluetooth device and play to another at the same time, and once the microphone and the speaker share a room, they feed each other.

The audio engine

Full-duplex and callback-driven, written in C++ on Oboe, with a lock-free ring buffer between the input and output callbacks. There is no allocation, no lock and no logging inside the audio callbacks. The buffer starts small, grows when the platform reports underruns, and shrinks back when things settle.

Echo cancellation, built from scratch

Android's own echo canceller is built for handset-length delays, not for a Bluetooth speaker 150 to 400 ms away. So the relay cancels its own loop in three stages:

  • Delay estimation: GCC-PHAT cross-correlation on a worker thread, which gives a sharp peak at the true delay even with music playing.
  • Adaptive filtering: a 768-tap NLMS filter predicts the echo and subtracts it. If it ever starts adding energy, adaptation freezes.
  • Double-talk protection: it tells the speaker's echo apart from a person talking, so it never removes the voice it is supposed to carry.

Status

The C++ core and the Android app are built and run. Latency figures in the screenshots were measured on the development device, which is slower than real hardware. The Bluetooth path still has to be validated on physical devices, so no Bluetooth performance is claimed here.
