LoRA training on FLUX.1 with Ostris AI Toolkit and on SD 1.5/SDXL with kohya_ss, ComfyUI workflows, Wan 2.1 text-to-video and script-to-talking-head video, built and tested in a real product. 10 business problems it solves.

Your brand needs product photos, a character or short videos that look the same every time, and stock AI tools keep drifting. I fix that by training a model on your own images (a LoRA), wiring it into ComfyUI or an API your team can call, and running it on GPUs you can afford. Everything on this page comes from a working product I built, FaceSearch, and its GPU engines.

10 problems this solves

1

Product photos without a photo shoot

Problem: every new product or season needs a new shoot.

What I build: a LoRA trained on 15 to 30 product photos (FLUX.1 or SDXL), then a ComfyUI graph that puts the product in any scene, light or angle.

Built on: the FaceSearch LoRA pipeline.

2

The same brand character in every campaign

Problem: AI images change the mascot or model's face on every image.

What I build: a character LoRA plus IP-Adapter FaceID and an OpenPose ControlNet, so identity and pose are both controlled.

Built on: ComfyUI consistent-face workflows.

3

AI headshots and avatars inside your app

Problem: users want professional photos of themselves without a photographer.

What I build: upload a few selfies, a personal LoRA trains in the background, and the user gets styled headshots. Training jobs are queued and tracked.

Built on: FaceSearch.

4

Virtual try-on for a fashion store

Problem: shoppers can't see how a garment looks on a person, and returns cost money.

What I build: garment photo + person photo in, try-on image out, with a provider router that falls back when one model fails.

Built on: FaceSearch Outfit Studio (demos on the case study).

5

Short marketing clips from a text prompt

Problem: social media needs fresh B-roll every week, and stock footage looks like stock.

What I build: a text-to-video engine (Wan 2.1) behind an API, prompts saved as templates, clips queued and stored.

Built on: the video engine above, verified on a T4.

6

A spokesperson video from a script

Problem: recording a founder or presenter for every update is slow.

What I build: type the script, pick a voice, get a lip-synced talking-head video. Only faces with recorded consent are accepted.

Built on: the talking-head engine above.

7

Blog posts and case studies turned into videos

Problem: good written content never reaches video platforms.

What I build: a pipeline that cuts a page into short beats, voices them, adds the page's images and renders a captioned video in 9:16, 1:1 or 16:9 plus post text. It only reuses sentences from the page, so it never makes claims you didn't approve.

Built on: the marketing module of the same video engine.

8

A ComfyUI workflow your app can call

Problem: the designer's ComfyUI graph works on one PC and nowhere else.

What I build: the graph becomes an API with a job queue, retries and stored results, so your web or mobile app can use it.

Built on: genflow, durable ComfyUI jobs in Go.

9

GPU costs that don't eat the margin

Problem: a GPU server running all month for a few jobs a day.

What I build: engines that run on rented GPUs, Colab or Kaggle only when there is work, each behind its own token, with training and generation queued.

Built on: the FaceSearch GPU engines, tested end to end on Kaggle.

10

Models you can legally sell with

Problem: many open models are free to try but not licensed for commercial use.

What I build: a licence check of every model and every hidden dependency before launch. In FaceSearch this found a non-commercial human-parser inside a "free" try-on model, so paying traffic goes to licensed providers.

Built on: the FaceSearch licence review.

A note on FLUX.1: FLUX.1 [dev] gives the best quality, but its weights have a non-commercial licence. For a product you sell, I plan around FLUX.1 [schnell], FLUX.2 klein 4B or SDXL, or a commercial licence from the model's maker. I tell you which one before any training starts.

How a project runs

  1. You send 10 to 30 sample images and the shots or clips you need.
  2. I pick the model and check its licence, and tell you the GPU cost per image or clip.
  3. First LoRA and test outputs in a few days, so you judge real results before the full build.
  4. Delivery: a ComfyUI graph, an API, or a page inside your app, with a short guide for your team.
