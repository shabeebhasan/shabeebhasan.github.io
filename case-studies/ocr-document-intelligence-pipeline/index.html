OCR That Outlived Its Rewrite Cycles

A document-scan pipeline maintained for six years for one client, plus a Tesseract / Google Cloud Vision OCR API for business cards, extraction work measured in years of production, not demos.

ocrtesseractgoogle cloud visiondocument extractionpython

The Brief

Two clients, one theme: turn messy real-world documents into structured data and keep doing it reliably. One needed business cards parsed into clean contact records; the other needed a document-scan pipeline that their operation could depend on for years.

What I Built

  • An OCR API combining Tesseract with Google Cloud Vision, using each engine where it wins, with cleanup and field-structuring logic on top so output is data, not text soup.
  • A document-scan pipeline that entered production in 2020 and stayed under my maintenance until 2026, six years of format drift, edge cases and quiet fixes.
  • Validation and exception paths: when confidence is low the document routes to a human, because a wrong extraction is worse than a slow one.

Why It Holds Up

OCR demos are easy; OCR operations are not. The engineering that matters is in the failure paths (confidence thresholds, exception queues, format drift) and that is exactly the part that kept these clients for years.
